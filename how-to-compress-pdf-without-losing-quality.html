How to compress a PDF without losing quality

A big PDF is hard to email and often rejected by upload forms. Here is how to shrink it and still keep it readable.

Updated 11 November 2026

Why are some PDFs so large?

The biggest reason is pictures. A scan or a phone photo saved as a PDF holds a large image on every page. Ten pages of scans can easily pass 20 MB. PDFs made from text, such as a Word document, are usually small.

Fonts and old edits stored inside the file can also add weight, but for most people the pictures are the problem.

How to compress a PDF with Invatek

  1. Open the Compress PDF tool and choose your file.
  2. Pick a level. Balanced is the best start, Strong gives the smallest file, and Light keeps the text but saves less.
  3. Tap Compress PDF. The tool shows the old size and the new size.
  4. Save the new file and open it to check that the pages are clear.

Which level should I choose?

  • Balanced: pages are redrawn at a medium quality. Good for documents you will read on screen.
  • Strong: the smallest file. Pages become pictures, so text cannot be selected or searched. Use it when a form has a strict size limit.
  • Light: keeps text as text and makes small savings. It helps most with files that were never optimised.

Good habits

Compress once from your original. Compressing a compressed file again only lowers the quality more. If a file is a scan, scanning it in black and white at a sensible size often gives a smaller PDF in the first place.

Always open the result before you send it, and keep the original in case you need better quality later.

Questions

Why did my file not get smaller?

Some PDFs are already well compressed. The tool then returns your original and tells you so. Try the Strong level.

Can I still search the text?

Not after Balanced or Strong, because pages become pictures. Light keeps text.
