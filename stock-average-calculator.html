About the Stock Average Calculator

If you buy the same share on different days at different prices, your real cost per share is not any one of those prices. It is the weighted average: total money spent divided by total shares held. That figure is what you compare with the market price to know if you are in profit.

Buying more of a share you already hold when the price has fallen is called averaging down. It pulls your average cost lower, so the share has to recover less for you to break even. It also puts more money into the same company, so it is a decision to make with care, not a trick that removes the risk.

How to use it

  1. Enter the quantity and the price for each purchase. Tap "Add another buy" for more rows.
  2. Your average buy price, total quantity and total money invested appear at once.
  3. Use "Thinking of buying more?" to test a purchase before you make it.
  4. Use the target box to find out how many shares you would need at a given price to bring the average down to a number you want.
  5. Enter today's price in the last box to see profit or loss.

An example

Example. You bought 100 shares at ₹250 and 50 shares at ₹200. Total spent: ₹35,000 for 150 shares. Average: ₹233.33. If you now buy 50 more at ₹180, the average comes down to ₹220. To bring it all the way down to ₹210 by buying at ₹180 you would need 117 shares, which is ₹21,060 of fresh money.

Tips

  • Do not average down just to "feel better". Ask if you would buy this share today at today's price.
  • Averaging does not change the fact that you hold more of one company. Keep an eye on how much of your money sits in one share.
  • The rows are saved on this device so you can come back later. Use Clear all to start fresh.

Questions

How is the average price of shares calculated?

Multiply quantity by price for each buy, add them all up, then divide by the total number of shares.

What does averaging down mean?

It means buying more of a share after its price has dropped so that your average cost per share goes down.

Does this include brokerage and charges?

No. Add those costs into your buy price if you want them counted.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The rows are kept in your browser on this device only.
